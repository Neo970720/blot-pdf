Three steps, one tab

A redaction is only real when the text under it is gone

Most online tools put a black rectangle over the words and save the file — the words are still there, selectable and searchable. Blot replaces the page with an image and paints the boxes into it.

01

Open the file

Drop a PDF in. It is read by the browser itself, so nothing travels over the network — you can do this with Wi-Fi switched off.

02

Mark what must go

Drag boxes over names, numbers, clauses — or let Pro scan the text layer for emails, cards, SSNs, IBANs and your own keyword list.

03

Export, then prove it

The export is a new document at 150–300 DPI with metadata stripped. Blot reopens it and searches for every value you redacted before telling you it passed.

The part everyone skips

You cannot see a failed redaction by looking at the page

A 2019 court filing taught the whole industry this: black bars in a PDF can sit on top of text that is still there. The only proof is opening the exported file and searching it. Blot does that for you, in the same tab, before you download.

What the check does
Reopens the exported bytes, extracts the text layer of every page, and searches for every value the tool redacted — auto-detected matches and the text that sat under your manual boxes.
What a pass means
Zero hits, and rasterised pages carry no extractable text at all. The report shows page count, resolution and the SHA-256 of the exact file you received.
What it cannot do
It cannot see text baked into a scan as pixels — that needs OCR, which this tool does not run. Draw your boxes on scans and the page is burned as an image either way.

Questions people actually ask

Short answers

Does Blot upload my PDF?

No. Blot is a static site. The PDF is read, redacted and written again inside your browser tab. There is no endpoint that accepts files, and the app keeps working after you switch off your connection.

Is this real redaction or just a black box drawn on top?

Real redaction. Any page with a redaction is converted to an image at 150–300 DPI and the boxes are painted into that image, so the text underneath no longer exists in the file. Pages without redactions are copied as vectors with annotations removed, and the export is written as a new document, so bookmarks, form fields and attachments from the original are not carried over.

How do I know the redaction worked?

The app reopens the file it just produced and searches the text layer for every value it redacted. The result appears in the report panel with the page count, resolution and SHA-256 of the output. You can run the same check in any PDF reader by searching for the redacted word.

What does it cost?

Free covers files up to 10 pages with manual redactions at 150 DPI, including the leak check, with no account. Pro is $9 per month or $29 once and adds unlimited pages, batch processing, PII auto-detect, 300 DPI, box labels and a redaction certificate.

Does it work on scanned PDFs?

Yes for redacting them: you draw boxes over the scan and the page is rasterised, so those pixels are gone. Automatic detection needs a text layer, so on a pure scan you mark the boxes yourself (or run OCR elsewhere first).

What happens to my licence when I go offline?

A licence key is checked once against Polar and then cached in this browser, so Pro keeps working offline. It is re-checked in the background at most once a week when you are online.
